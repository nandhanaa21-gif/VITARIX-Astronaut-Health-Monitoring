Sleep and Recovery Guidance

General educational suggestions for maintaining a consistent rest routine during a mission.

Maintain a regular sleep and wake schedule whenever mission operations allow.
Reduce unnecessary light and noise during the planned rest period.
Review repeated reports of poor sleep with the appropriate mission health professional.

SPACEFLIGHT HEALTH AWARENESS

The Five Major Hazards of Human Spaceflight

Key spaceflight hazards and the health areas that may need attention.

☢️ 1. Space Radiation

What it is: Exposure to energetic particles and radiation in space.

Possible health effects: Increased long-term cancer risk and potential effects on the central nervous system and other tissues.

Monitoring considerations: Mission radiation exposure records, shielding conditions and health follow-up.

🧑‍🚀 2. Isolation and Confinement

What it is: Living and working in a small, enclosed environment, often with a limited crew.

Possible health effects: Stress, changes in mood, sleep disruption and interpersonal challenges.

Monitoring considerations: Mood, stress level, sleep quality and crew well-being check-ins.

🌍 3. Distance from Earth

What it is: Long communication delays and limited access to immediate support or supplies.

Possible health effects: Delayed medical support, increased operational stress and challenges responding to emergencies.

Monitoring considerations: Health trends, symptom reporting, medical resources and communication plans.

🛰️ 4. Gravity Fields

What it is: Exposure to microgravity during spaceflight and gravity changes during transitions.

Possible health effects: Bone and muscle loss, balance changes and fluid shifts.

Monitoring considerations: Exercise, physical performance, balance symptoms and relevant medical assessments.

🧪 5. Hostile / Closed Environments

What it is: Dependence on a controlled spacecraft environment with limited resources and potential equipment or environmental hazards.

Possible health effects: Effects associated with air quality, temperature, noise, contaminants or equipment failures.

Monitoring considerations: Cabin environmental systems, air quality, temperature, noise and crew-reported symptoms.

Educational information only. These hazards are not directly measured by the current VITARIX demo. The dashboard uses manually submitted health information and does not replace mission medical procedures.
